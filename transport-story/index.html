Australia's Public Transport Journey

How people use buses, trains, trams and ferries, and how public transport use has changed across Australian cities.

Explore how Australians travel by public transport and how use has changed over time. These charts compare city patronage, transport modes and everyday commuting choices. Census figures describe travel to work on one day, while patronage data tracks trips across each year.

Public Transport Recovery Across Eight Cities

Each circle marks a capital city. Larger circles represent more public transport trips in 2025. Colour compares each city with its 2019 patronage level. Blue means trips have passed that level, while red means they remain below it. Hover to see the values.

Public Transport Commuting Across States

Each state is shaded by the share of employed people who travelled to work by public transport on Census day in 2021. Darker states had a larger share. These figures are a snapshot from a period when several cities were in lockdown.

Commuting by Transit and Working from Home

This map compares public transport commuting with working from home in each state. Blue tones show higher public transport shares, and red tones show higher work from home shares. Mixed dark tones indicate higher shares for both choices. Colours group states into three levels for each measure.

The Pandemic Shock and the Return of Riders

Each square compares one city's public transport trips in a year with its 2019 level. A value of 100 means the city had the same number of trips. The figures include trains, trams, buses and ferries.

Melbourne fell furthest, to 35 in the 2020 to 2021 financial year. Sydney, Brisbane and Canberra reached their lowest point a year later. By the 2024 to 2025 financial year, Perth (105), Brisbane (100) and Canberra (100) were back at or above their 2019 level. Melbourne was still at 82.

Which Cities Recovered First?

Each year, the eight cities are ranked by how close their trips are to their 2019 level. Rank 1 is closest to that level and rank 8 is furthest away. Lines moving toward rank 1 show cities catching up.

Five Decades of Public Transport Patronage

Each band represents one type of public transport. Its thickness shows the number of trips across the eight capital cities each year, in millions.

Car Commuting in Capital Cities and Regional Areas

Each row compares the share of workers who drove in a capital city with the share in the rest of its state. The dots show the two values, and the line shows the difference between them. The Northern Territory has the largest contrast, while Western Australia is close to even.

Do Commuting Habits Align with City Recovery?

Each line connects a city's state rank for public transport commuting with its capital city rank for patronage recovery. Rank 1 is highest on each measure. Lines that change position show where the rankings differ. The measures cover different years and geographic areas, so this chart compares patterns without showing cause and effect.

The Modes Behind City Travel

Each block represents one transport mode in one city. Larger blocks show modes with more trips in 2025. Hover to see the values.

How Australians Travel to Work Across States

Each line represents a state across four ways of travelling to work. Every column uses its own scale, with states positioned from the lowest to the highest share. Read the labels at the ends of each scale to compare values.

A Hundred Workers and Their Commute Choices

Each square represents one employed person in the 2021 Census. The squares show how people travelled to work, worked from home or did not go to work on Census day.
