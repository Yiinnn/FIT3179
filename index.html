Australia has eight capital city networks.

Each lost riders during COVID lockdowns.

Some recovered quickly. Others are catching up.

The page uses two kinds of data.

BITRE counts trips across a whole year.

The Census records one travel day.

Each source answers a different question.

Finish with commuting choices today.

Reading the dates

The Census records one day: 10 August 2021.

COVID restrictions applied in several cities then.

The pandemic shock and return of riders

Every city fell in 2020–21.

Melbourne fell furthest, to 35.

A value of 100 matches 2018–19.

  • Sydney, Brisbane and Canberra fell lowest later.
  • Their lows were 43, 58 and 60.
  • By 2024–25 Perth reached 105.
  • Brisbane reached 100; Canberra reached 100.
  • Melbourne remained at 82.
Read more

Before 2020, trips stayed fairly steady.

Most cities ranged between 85 and 105.

Lockdowns then cut ridership everywhere.

Melbourne recorded the deepest fall.

The pattern does not prove a cause.

Recovery then split the cities.

Perth never fell below 72.

It returned to 100 first.

Melbourne and Darwin remain below 100.

Five decades of growth, one sharp pandemic dip

Each band represents one transport mode.

Thickness shows annual trips, in millions.

The dashed line marks the lockdown period.

  • Trips grew toward a 2019 peak.
  • That peak was just under 1,800 million.
  • Trips halved across 2020–21 and 2021–22.
  • In 2025, rail reached 682m; bus 670m.
  • Light rail reached 220m; ferry 28m.
Read more

The long view shows an unusual decline.

Growth had been steady for decades.

Heavy rail and bus carry most trips.

Light rail and ferry are smaller.

Ferry is difficult to see clearly.

Use the legend to identify each band.

By 2025, total trips neared 1,600 million.

That remains below the 2019 peak.
