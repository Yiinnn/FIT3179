Perth and Brisbane have passed pre-pandemic levels

Each circle represents a capital city.

Size shows trips in 2024–25.

Colour compares recovery with 2018–19.

  • Perth leads at 105.1% of its baseline.
  • Brisbane follows at 100.2%.
  • Canberra is close behind at 99.6%.
  • Adelaide is 91.4%; Sydney is 90.8%.
  • Darwin is 83.1%; Hobart is 82.6%.
  • Melbourne is lowest at 82.0%.

Public transport commuting varies between local areas

Each area shows workers using public transport only.

Darker areas have a higher share.

The scale runs from 0% to 25%.

  • The map covers 2,450 local areas.
  • Most areas use the lightest shades.
  • Highest shares cluster around capital cities.
  • Rural and outback shares are very low.

More public transport commuters does not always mean more working from home

Each state combines two employment shares.

Darker tones mean higher values on both.

Hover for exact shares.

  • Public transport groups: under 3%, 3–under 5%, 5%+.
  • Work from home groups: under 15%, 15–under 25%, 25%+.
  • WA leads public transport, at about 7%.
  • NSW leads work from home, at about 31%.
  • NT is low on both: about 3% and 4%.

The pandemic shock and return of riders

Every city fell in 2020–21.

Melbourne fell furthest, to 35.

A value of 100 matches 2018–19.

  • Sydney, Brisbane and Canberra fell lowest later.
  • Their lows were 43, 58 and 60.
  • By 2024–25 Perth reached 105.
  • Brisbane reached 100; Canberra reached 100.
  • Melbourne remained at 82.

Which cities recovered first?

Each year ranks the eight cities.

Rank 1 is closest to baseline.

Lines moving up show cities catching up.

  • Perth reaches rank 1 by 2023–24.
  • Brisbane climbs to rank 2 by 2024–25.
  • Melbourne finishes last.

Five decades of growth, one sharp pandemic dip

Each band represents one transport mode.

Thickness shows annual trips, in millions.

The dashed line marks the lockdown period.

  • Trips grew toward a 2019 peak.
  • That peak was just under 1,800 million.
  • Trips halved across 2020–21 and 2021–22.
  • In 2025, rail reached 682m; bus 670m.
  • Light rail reached 220m; ferry 28m.

Car commuting in capital cities and regional areas

Each row compares city and regional drivers.

Dots show each share.

The line shows the difference.

  • The NT is the only reverse pattern.
  • About 69% drive in Darwin, 54% regionally.
  • NSW has the largest gap the other way.
  • About 35% drive in Sydney, 61% regionally.
  • WA is near even, at about 63%.

Do commuting habits align with city recovery?

Lines link commute and recovery ranks.

Rank 1 is highest on each measure.

Crossings show where ranks differ.

  • Perth ranks first on both measures.
  • Darwin and Hobart are near the bottom.
  • Brisbane ranks fifth for commuting, second for recovery.
  • Melbourne ranks fourth for commuting, last for recovery.

How Australians travel to work across states

Each panel shows one travel mode.

Bars show employed people’s shares.

Each panel has its own scale.

  • Car use ranges from 47% in NSW.
  • It reaches about 71% in the NT.
  • Public transport peaks in WA, near 7%.
  • NT walking and cycling reach about 10%.
  • NSW work from home reaches about 31%.
